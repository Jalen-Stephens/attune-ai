## When to use

Use this when conversations tend to turn into arguments because one or both partners feel unheard. It helps when you want to slow down and show the other person you are trying to understand before responding or defending.

## Steps

1. **Pause before replying.** When your partner finishes a sentence, take a breath. Avoid jumping in with your view, your story, or a fix right away.

2. **Reflect back what you heard.** In your own words, summarize the main point or feeling: “So you’re saying that when I work late, you feel left out?” or “It sounds like you’re frustrated because this keeps happening.”

3. **Check if you got it right.** Ask: “Did I get that right?” or “Is there more?” This gives your partner a chance to correct or add. You are not agreeing yet—you are showing you are listening.

4. **Wait for a signal before sharing your side.** When your partner feels understood, they often soften. Then you can ask: “Can I share how it looks from my side?” and take turns.

5. **Practice in low-stakes moments.** Try reflective listening when the topic is not highly charged so it feels more natural when things are tense.

## Notes

- Reflective listening does not mean you agree. It means you are demonstrating that you heard the other person.
- If you get it wrong, your partner can clarify. The goal is understanding, not perfect paraphrasing.
- This is communication coaching only, not a substitute for couples or individual therapy when needed.
