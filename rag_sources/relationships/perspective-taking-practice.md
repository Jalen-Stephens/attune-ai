## When to use

Use this when you are stuck in “I’m right, they’re wrong” or when you notice you keep explaining your view without really considering your partner’s. It helps in ongoing conflicts where both people feel misunderstood.

## Steps

1. **Pick one recent disagreement.** Choose something specific, not the whole relationship. Write or say in one sentence what you wanted or believed in that situation.

2. **State your partner’s view as they might.** In 2–3 sentences, describe their position or concern as if you were them. “From their side, they probably feel that…” or “They might be thinking that…” Do this even if you disagree.

3. **Name one thing that could be valid in their view.** You do not have to give up your position. Just identify one piece that makes sense from their perspective: “I can see why they’d feel dismissed if I was on my phone.”

4. **Share with your partner (optional).** If it feels safe, you can say: “I’ve been trying to see it from your side. Here’s what I think you might be feeling…” and invite them to correct or add.

5. **Invite them to try the same.** Ask: “Would you be willing to try describing how you think it looks from my side? I’ll listen and then we can clarify.”

## Notes

- Perspective-taking does not mean you agree or that your needs do not matter. It can create space for both views to exist before problem-solving.
- If the relationship has a lot of blame or hurt, this practice may work better with a therapist or coach guiding the conversation.
- This is communication and relationship coaching only, not a substitute for therapy or crisis support.
