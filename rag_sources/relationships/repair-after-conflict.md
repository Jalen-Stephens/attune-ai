## When to use

Use this after an argument or hurtful exchange when you want to reconnect instead of staying distant or pretending nothing happened. It helps when one or both of you said things you regret or when the air still feels heavy.

## Steps

1. **Wait until you are both calmer.** Repair works better when neither person is in fight-or-flight mode. If needed, agree on a time to talk later: “I don’t want to drop this. Can we talk in an hour after we’ve both cooled down?”

2. **Take responsibility for your part.** Name what you did without piling on excuses: “I raised my voice and that made it harder for you to hear me” or “I shut down and left the room instead of staying.”

3. **Acknowledge the impact.** Show you get how it landed: “I know that felt dismissive” or “I can see why you felt alone in that moment.”

4. **Say what you will try differently.** One small, concrete thing: “Next time I’ll try to say I need a break instead of walking out” or “I’ll work on not interrupting when you’re explaining.”

5. **Invite the other person to share.** Ask: “What do you need from me right now?” or “Is there anything else you want me to understand?” Then listen without defending.

## Notes

- Repair does not require both people to apologize at once. One person can start; the other can respond when ready.
- If the same conflicts keep repeating, consider whether deeper patterns or outside support (e.g., couples work) would help.
- This is communication and relationship coaching only, not therapy or crisis intervention.
