## When to use

Use this when you need to bring up something sensitive—money, parenting, in-laws, trust, or unmet needs—and you want to increase the chance it is heard instead of turning into a blow-up or shutdown.

## Steps

1. **Choose timing.** Ask when your partner has capacity: “I’d like to talk about something that’s been on my mind. Is now okay, or can we set a time in the next day or two?” Avoid starting when they are walking out the door or right before sleep.

2. **Name the topic in one line.** “I want to talk about how we handle disagreements” or “I’d like to discuss our budget and some worries I have.” That way the other person is not guessing what is coming.

3. **State your intention.** “I’m not trying to blame you—I want us to figure this out together” or “I need to say something that’s hard for me, and I’m hoping you can hear me.”

4. **Keep it to one main issue per conversation.** If you pile on several topics, the conversation can spiral. You can schedule another talk for other issues.

5. **Agree on a signal to pause.** “If either of us gets too upset, we’ll take a 10-minute break and come back.” That can prevent saying things you regret and make it safer to continue later.

## Notes

- The goal is to open the conversation, not to win or to get immediate agreement. Listening and being heard often take more than one talk.
- If your partner is not ready, you can say: “I hear you’re not ready. When would be a better time?” and try again.
- This is communication support only; it does not replace professional help in situations involving abuse or serious mental health concerns.
