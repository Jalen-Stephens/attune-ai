## When to use

Use this when you notice you are upset but have not clearly said what you need, or when your partner often says they do not know what you want. It helps when you want to move from blame (“You never…”) to clear, askable requests.

## Steps

1. **Identify the situation.** In one or two sentences, describe what happened without judging: “When we come home from work, we go straight to our devices” or “When we disagree, we raise our voices.”

2. **Name the feeling.** What do you feel in that situation? Sad, lonely, dismissed, anxious, angry? One or two words is enough.

3. **Connect to a need.** Under the feeling is often a need: to be included, to feel safe, to be respected, to have time together. Finish the sentence: “I need…” or “It matters to me that…”

4. **Turn the need into a request.** Ask for something concrete and doable: “Can we put phones away for the first 20 minutes after we get home?” or “Can we agree to take a break if either of us raises our voice?”

5. **Keep the request specific and time-bound when possible.** “I’d like us to talk about money once a week for 15 minutes” is easier to try than “I want you to care more about our budget.”

## Notes

- Needs are not demands. You can state what you need and still accept that the other person may say no or suggest something different.
- If your partner is not used to this language, you can share that you are trying to ask more clearly and would like their help.
- This is for improving communication; it does not replace professional support for abuse or serious conflict.
