## When to use

Use this when you find yourself replaying the day, worrying about tomorrow, or unable to turn off thoughts once you are in bed. It is for people who feel physically tired but mentally alert at bedtime.

## Steps

1. **Acknowledge without fighting.** Tell yourself something like: “My mind is active right now.” Pushing thoughts away often makes them stick around longer. Brief acknowledgment can reduce the struggle.

2. **Schedule a short “worry window” earlier in the day.** Set 10–15 minutes in the afternoon or early evening to write or think about concerns. When thoughts come at night, remind yourself you already have a time for that.

3. **Use a simple anchor.** Focus on your breath, the weight of the blanket, or the sound of a fan. When you notice you have drifted into thinking, gently return to the anchor without judging yourself.

4. **Get out of bed if you are stuck.** If you have been awake for 20–30 minutes and your mind is still racing, get up. Do something calm and dim (e.g., read, listen to something boring) until you feel drowsy, then go back to bed.

5. **Keep a notepad by the bed.** If a thought feels urgent (“I must remember to…”), write it down in one line. That can help the mind let go so you can return to focusing on sleep.

## Notes

- Racing thoughts are common and do not mean something is wrong with you. Many people benefit from these kinds of habits.
- If the same worries come up night after night, consider whether they need daytime attention (e.g., problem-solving, boundaries, or support).
- This is supportive guidance only, not treatment for anxiety or other conditions.
