## When to use

Use this when you want to review and adjust your bedroom so it supports sleep rather than alertness. Helpful when you have not looked at your sleep environment in a while or when you have moved or changed your routine.

## Steps

1. **Light.** Check whether streetlights, devices, or early morning sun enter the room. Consider blackout curtains, an eye mask, or moving or covering small LEDs. In the hour before bed, use dim or warm lighting where possible.

2. **Noise.** Notice if sounds wake you or keep you from falling asleep. Options include earplugs, a white-noise machine, or a fan. Test a few options to see what feels comfortable.

3. **Temperature.** Many people sleep better in a slightly cool room. If you often wake too warm or too cold, adjust bedding or room temperature in small steps.

4. **Bed use.** Use the bed mainly for sleep (and intimacy if applicable). If you often work, watch TV, or scroll in bed, try moving those activities elsewhere so the bed becomes a strong cue for sleep.

5. **Comfort.** Notice your mattress, pillow, and bedding. You do not need to buy new things immediately; small changes (e.g., pillow position, an extra blanket) can sometimes help.

## Notes

- Change one or two things at a time so you can see what helps. You do not need a perfect setup.
- If you share the room, talk with your partner about what each of you needs (e.g., darkness, noise level) and find compromises.
- This is general coaching only; it does not replace medical or sleep-specialist advice.
