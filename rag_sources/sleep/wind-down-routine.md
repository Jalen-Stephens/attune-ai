## When to use

Use this when you want to create a consistent pre-sleep routine so your body and mind learn to associate certain actions with sleep. It helps when you lie in bed with your mind still racing or when you are unsure what to do in the hour before bed.

## Steps

1. **Pick a target bedtime** and work backward. Decide when you want to be in bed with lights off, then set a wind-down start time 45–60 minutes before that.

2. **Choose 3–5 low-stimulation activities** that feel calming to you. Examples: dimming lights, reading (paper or e-ink), light stretching, a warm shower or bath, a caffeine-free drink, or quiet music.

3. **Avoid screens and heavy mental load** in the last 30–45 minutes. If you use a phone, turn on night mode and avoid scrolling, work, or intense content.

4. **Do the same sequence in the same order** most nights. Consistency helps your nervous system recognize that sleep is coming.

5. **Keep the routine short enough that you can stick to it.** Start with 20–30 minutes if a full hour feels like too much.

## Notes

- The goal is predictability, not perfection. Missing a night or shortening the routine is okay; resume the next night.
- If you notice that a specific activity revs you up (e.g., certain music or topics), move it earlier in the evening or drop it from the wind-down block.
- Over time, your body may start to feel drowsier as you begin the routine, which is a sign that the habit is working.
