## When to use

Use this when you want to understand what tends to come before a craving or slip so you can plan ahead. It helps when you notice the same situations, people, or feelings showing up before you act.

## Steps

1. **Pick one recent time** when you had a strong urge or acted on it. Write or say what was going on: time of day, place, who you were with, what had happened earlier.

2. **Notice what was going on inside.** What were you feeling? Stressed, lonely, bored, angry, sad, anxious? What were you thinking? “I deserve this,” “Just this once,” “I can’t stand this”?

3. **Look for patterns.** Over the last few times, what was similar? Same day of the week? After work? After a fight? When you were alone? When you were celebrating? Patterns are not excuses—they are information.

4. **Name your top 2–3 triggers.** Write them simply: “Sunday evenings when I’m alone,” “After I argue with my partner,” “When I feel like a failure.” You do not need a long list; a few clear ones are enough.

5. **Plan one small step for each.** For each trigger, what could you do differently next time? Examples: “On Sunday evenings I’ll call a friend or go to a meeting,” “After an argument I’ll take a walk before I do anything else.”

## Notes

- Trigger mapping is not about blaming yourself. It is about seeing the conditions that make urges more likely so you can prepare.
- Plans do not have to be perfect. One realistic option per trigger is a good start.
- This is supportive coaching only; it does not replace treatment or professional support for addiction.
