## When to use

Use this when you know a situation is coming that usually brings strong urges—a party, a holiday, a stressful meeting, or time alone—and you want to plan how you will handle it instead of winging it.

## Steps

1. **Name the situation.** Be specific: “New Year’s party at my friend’s house,” “Friday night when my partner is away,” “Right after I get my paycheck.”

2. **Imagine yourself in it.** Close your eyes or picture it. Where are you? Who is there? What do you see, hear, feel? Notice what might make the urge stronger—people, places, smells, emotions.

3. **Decide what you will do.** Choose 2–3 concrete actions: “I will bring a plus-one who knows my goals,” “I will leave by 10 p.m.,” “I will text my sponsor before I go and when I arrive.” Write them down.

4. **Decide what you will do if the urge hits anyway.** “If I feel a strong urge, I will go to the bathroom, breathe for 2 minutes, then text my support person. If it doesn’t ease, I will leave.”

5. **Review the plan once before the situation.** The day before or the morning of, read your plan again. You have already decided; in the moment you just follow it.

## Notes

- Coping ahead does not guarantee you will not slip. It increases the chance you will have a clear path when the situation arrives.
- If the situation changes (e.g., someone cancels, you feel sick), adjust the plan. The skill is planning, not sticking to a rigid script.
- This is supportive coaching only, not treatment. Professional support can help with ongoing relapse prevention.
