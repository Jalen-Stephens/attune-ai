## When to use

Use this when you feel a strong urge or craving and want to avoid acting on it immediately. It helps when you notice the urge building and want to ride it out instead of fighting it or giving in right away.

## Steps

1. **Pause and notice.** When you feel the urge, stop what you are doing if you can. Take a breath. Name it: “This is an urge. It’s strong right now.”

2. **Observe without acting.** Notice where you feel it in your body—chest, stomach, throat. Notice the intensity. Does it feel like a wave? Urges often peak and then ease if you do not feed them.

3. **Breathe through it.** Keep breathing at a steady pace. You are not trying to make the urge go away; you are letting it be there while you stay still. Imagine the urge as a wave that rises and then falls.

4. **Remind yourself it will pass.** Urges are time-limited. Even when they feel overwhelming, they usually peak within several minutes if you do not act. “I can wait this out. I don’t have to do anything right now.”

5. **Do something else after it eases.** Once the intensity drops, move: call someone, take a short walk, drink water, or do a simple task. You have just practiced not acting on the urge.

## Notes

- You do not have to like the urge or feel calm. The goal is to not act on it for a few minutes, then a few more.
- If you act on the urge, that does not erase the practice. Notice what happened and what you might do differently next time.
- This is supportive coaching only, not treatment for addiction or substance use. Professional support can help with ongoing recovery.
