## When to use

Use this when a craving hits and you want a simple structure to get through the next 15–30 minutes without acting. It helps when you know that if you can buy a little time, the urge often weakens.

## Steps

1. **Commit to a short delay.** Tell yourself: “I am not going to act on this for the next 15 minutes.” You are not promising forever—just this window. Set a timer if that helps.

2. **Leave the trigger environment if you can.** If you are in a place or with people that make the urge stronger, move. Go to another room, step outside, or take a short walk. Changing the scene can interrupt the automatic pull.

3. **Use your body.** Do something that uses your body: walk, stretch, cold water on your face or hands, squeeze a stress ball, or do a few jumping jacks. Physical activity can shift your state.

4. **Engage your mind.** Call or text someone, work on a puzzle, watch a short video, or read a few pages. Choose something that holds your attention enough to take the focus off the urge.

5. **When the timer goes off, decide again.** After 15 minutes, check in. If the urge is still strong, set another 15 minutes. If it has eased, you have just practiced delay—and you can repeat the same strategy next time.

## Notes

- Delay does not mean you are weak if you eventually act. Each delay is practice. Over time, delaying can become easier.
- Have a short list of go-to distractions (people, activities, places) so you do not have to think when the urge is high.
- This is supportive coaching only, not a substitute for professional addiction or mental health care.
