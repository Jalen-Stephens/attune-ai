## When to use

Use this when you have trouble staying on one task for long or when you tend to work until you burn out and then avoid the task. It helps when you want to work with your attention span instead of against it.

## Steps

1. **Choose one task.** Pick a single focus—one report, one section, one type of email. Not “catch up on work,” but something specific.

2. **Set a short timer.** Start with 15 or 20 minutes. You can increase later if it works. Tell yourself: “I only have to do this for 15 minutes.”

3. **Work until the timer goes off.** If you get distracted, notice and return to the task. You do not have to be perfect; the goal is to stay with the task for the sprint.

4. **When the timer goes off, stop.** Take a 5-minute break. Stand up, move, look away from the screen. Do not skip the break—it helps your brain reset.

5. **Repeat or switch.** Do another sprint on the same task or switch to something else. After 2–3 sprints, take a longer break (15–20 minutes).

## Notes

- Short sprints reduce the pressure to “focus for hours.” Many people find they get more done in a few 15-minute blocks than in one long, distracted session.
- If 15 minutes is too long, try 10. If 15 feels easy, try 25. Adjust to what works for you.
- This is practical coaching only, not a substitute for evaluation or treatment of ADHD or attention difficulties.
