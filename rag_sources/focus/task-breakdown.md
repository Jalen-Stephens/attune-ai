## When to use

Use this when a task feels too big to start or you keep putting it off because you do not know where to begin. It helps when you have a deadline or a goal but the first step is unclear.

## Steps

1. **Write the task in one sentence.** What is the outcome? “Finish the report” or “Clean the kitchen” or “Apply for the job.” Naming it clearly is the first step.

2. **List every small step.** Do not worry about order yet. Write down everything that has to happen: open the document, find the data, write the intro, write section 1, etc. Tiny steps are fine—even “open laptop” or “get a glass of water” if that helps you start.

3. **Put the steps in order.** Number them or arrange them so the first step is something you can do right now. The first step should be so small that it feels almost silly—that is the point.

4. **Do only the first step.** Set a timer for 5 or 10 minutes if that helps. You are not committing to the whole task—just this one step. When the step is done, you can stop or do the next one.

5. **Repeat.** When you get stuck again, break the current step down further or take a short break and come back to the next small step.

## Notes

- Breaking down tasks does not make them magically easy, but it reduces the “where do I start?” freeze.
- You do not need a perfect list. A rough list is better than no list. You can adjust as you go.
- This is practical coaching only, not a substitute for evaluation or treatment of ADHD or other conditions.
