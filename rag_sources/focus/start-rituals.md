## When to use

Use this when you have a task you need to do but keep avoiding it—starting feels harder than the task itself. It helps when you want a repeatable “on-ramp” so your brain learns: this sequence means work is starting.

## Steps

1. **Pick a task you often avoid.** Name it: “Opening my email,” “Writing the first paragraph,” “Doing my taxes.” Something that you know you need to do but keep putting off.

2. **Design a short ritual (2–5 minutes).** Choose 2–4 steps you do every time before that task. Examples: make tea, sit in the same chair, put phone in another room, open the document, set a 15-minute timer. The ritual should be the same each time.

3. **Do the ritual without requiring the task.** On days when you are not ready to work, still do the ritual. Sit down, open the document, set the timer—then you can get up if you want. The goal is to make the ritual feel automatic and low-pressure.

4. **After the ritual, do one tiny step.** Open the first email. Write one sentence. Fill in one field. You do not have to finish; you only have to start. Often starting is the hardest part.

5. **Repeat.** Over time, the ritual becomes a cue: “When I do this, work happens.” You can add or change steps as you learn what helps.

## Notes

- Rituals are not magic. They work by reducing the number of decisions you have to make before starting.
- If the ritual starts to feel like a burden, shorten it or change one step. Keep it simple enough that you will actually do it.
- This is practical coaching only, not treatment for ADHD or motivation disorders.
